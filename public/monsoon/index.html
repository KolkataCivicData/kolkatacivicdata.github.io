Data

Source: one-minute readings from Weather Union stations in Kolkata, 9 May to 8 Nov 2026. These are the summary tables behind every chart on this page:

  • daily_network.csv: citywide rain and temperature for each day
  • stations.csv: season totals and data-quality notes for each station
  • monthly_station.csv: monthly rain for each station
  • rain_by_hour.csv and temp_by_hour.csv: hour-of-day averages by month
  • station_days.csv: each weather station's own rain and temperature per day
  • rain_onset.csv: temperature change around the start of rain
How these numbers were made
  • Source: Weather Union one-minute readings for Kolkata, 9 May to 8 Nov 2026. Device times are IST; temperatures bottom out around 5 am and peak around 1 pm. Duplicate readings were removed.
  • Daily rain is the day's final rain accumulation, which resets at midnight. It matches the sum of the minute-by-minute rain readings.
  • Single minutes reporting more than 5 mm were removed from every total. That is a rate of 300 mm an hour, they arrive with no gap in the data before them, and the stations around them saw nothing similar, so they are treated as sensor glitches. The station table lists how much was removed at each station.
  • A "full day" means the station sent rain readings for at least 80% of its minutes (1,152 of 1,440). Daily and rainy-day figures use full days only. Season and monthly totals also count partial days, because the accumulation still records all rain up to the last reading.
  • Gauges whose daily rain from June to September barely moves with the median of the other stations (correlation below 0.3) are excluded from all rain figures. That removes Howrah, which logged heavy rain on days the rest of the city was dry and almost nothing in July and August, and Chandannagar, which looks blocked.
  • Gauges that read below 35% of the other stations' median in a whole monsoon month are kept but marked, because their totals are probably too low.
  • The rain figures above the station ranking are medians across stations, so a single faulty gauge cannot move them much. August and September totals are within a few percent of each other, so treat either as the wettest month.
  • Temperature and humidity come from six full weather stations: Behala, Bhowanipore, Kankurgachi, Salt Lake 2, Shibpur and Shrirampur. Daily highs and lows are taken from the minute readings after a 5-minute rolling median, on full days only. New Town's sensor reported impossible values and Barabazar Market reports mostly in daytime, so both are left out.
  • Wind is not shown. Readings disagree too much between stations to be trusted: some anemometers barely register any wind, while others report near-gale speeds for hours at a time.
  • Heat index is not shown. These sensors appear to read humidity high, and the heat-index formula magnifies that into implausible values.
